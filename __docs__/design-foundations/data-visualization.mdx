# Data visualization

Data visualization turns learning data into decisions. Teachers and administrators use our charts to see who needs help, what's working, and what to do next. These foundations apply to every chart, wherever it appears. For how headings and controls are laid out on a page, see [Data reports](?path=/docs/design-guidelines-patterns-data-reports--docs). ## Choosing a chart

Start from the question, then pick the simplest form that answers it.

| To show… | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A single value or metric | Number (stat) | A chart with one data point |
| Comparison across categories | Bar chart (horizontal for long labels) | Pie chart, 3D bars |
| Change over time | Line chart (bar chart for few, discrete periods) | Area chart with overlapping series |
| Parts of a whole | Stacked bar (100%) | Pie or donut with more than 3 slices |
| Progress toward a goal | Progress bar | Gauge or dial |
| Distribution across levels | Stacked bar or histogram | Line chart |
| Exact values or many dimensions | Table | A chart that needs a legend to decode |

### When not to chart

Use a number or a table instead when:

- There's only one value to show.
- Users need to look up or compare exact values.
- The data has more dimensions than one chart can show clearly.

## Color

Chart colors come from the color palette and are chosen by **role**: what the color does in the chart. Color supports meaning but never carries it alone.

### Color roles

Assessment, mastery, and motivation data each have a dedicated role. Every other chart uses the generic roles.

| Role | Use when | Colors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single series | One quantity on the chart. This is the default. | Blue 50, Blue 40 |
| Categorical | Two or more series the reader tracks separately | Cyan 40, Orange 40, Magenta 40, Green 40 |
| Neutral | A mark is context, not the subject | Gray 60 |
| Emphasis | One series is the point; the rest are comparison | Blue 50, Blue 40, Gray 30, Gray 60 |
| Attention | Items fall short of a threshold and need action | Yellow 30, Gray 60 |
| Annotation | Words or reference marks that aren't data | Gray 10, Gray 30 |
| Motivation | Motivation data | Magenta 30 |
| Assessment | Assessment results | TODO |
| Mastery | Mastery levels | TODO |

### Fills and outlines

Most chart fills are light and don't reach 3:1 contrast on their own. Every filled mark (bars, segments, areas, points) gets a **1px outline** in a darker step of the same color, and the outline carries the contrast. In line charts, draw the line in the role's outline color at 2px.

### Single series

Draw one quantity clearly. Color carries no information and isn't asked to. This covers most charts in the product: a distribution, a total over time, or progress toward a target.

Single series is for any data **outside** assessments, mastery, and motivation. Those have their own roles; don't use blue for them.

Give each series a stable color the reader can follow across the whole view. Use when two or more series share one plot area.

| 2 | Orange 40 | Orange 30 |

- Apply colors in order.
- Keep a series' color the same across every chart in the view.
- Past four series, fold the rest into "Other" (using Neutral) or use a table.

### Neutral

Let a mark be present and readable without competing for attention. Use Neutral when a series is context rather than subject, when a long tail is folded into "Other," or for the midpoint of a diverging scale.

Answer one question about one series while keeping the rest visible as a yardstick. Use when the chart makes a single point, like "this one moved" or "this one lags."

### Attention

Mark the items that need someone to do something, and only those. Use when there's a threshold, target, or floor that some items fall short of, and the reader's task is to act on the shortfall.

<div style={{display: "flex", flexWrap: "wrap", gap: "16px", marginBlockEnd: "24px"}}>
    <Color title="Yellow 30" hex="#FCB706" />
    <Color title="Yellow 10" hex="#966B00" />
    <Color title="Gray 60" hex="#CBCBCD" />
    <Color title="Gray 30" hex="#8A8B90" />
</div>

| Items | Fill | Outline and line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Below the threshold | Yellow 30 | Yellow 10 |
| At or above the threshold | Gray 60 | Gray 30 |

Use Attention only when one direction is really bad. If it's simply a comparison, use Emphasis.

### Annotation

Let the chart say something in words or reference marks without that ink reading as data. Examples: a target line, a grade-level or district average, a callout explaining a drop, or a "term started here" marker.

### Number formatting

| Rule | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Round to the precision that matters for the decision | 82%, not 82.37% |
| Use counts when the reader acts on individuals | "23 of 28 students completed it" |
| Use percentages to compare groups of different sizes | "82% of Period 1 vs. 64% of Period 3" |
| Pair them when both matter | "82% (23 of 28)" |
| Abbreviate large numbers | 1.2K, 3.4M |
| Always include units | 45 min, 12 skills |
| Use relative dates within 7 days | "2 days ago," "Due Friday" |
| Use full dates beyond 7 days | Oct 15, 2026 |

### Missing and zero values

Zero and missing are different and must look different.

- **Zero** is a real value. Show it as `0`.
- **Missing** means no data exists. Show it as `—` and explain why in a tooltip or footnote.

## Writing for data

- **Write chart titles as insights.** The title directly above a chart states the takeaway: "Students are moving up, but five have never left Beginning," not "Mastery levels." A section heading above the chart container can name the topic, like "Growth over time."
- **Use plain language.** Avoid jargon like "cohort" or "variance" unless the audience expects it.
- **Name people consistently.** Use "students" for learners in a class context.
- **Label directly.** Put labels on the marks instead of relying on a legend.

## Accessibility

### Color must not be the only signal

Pair color with direct labels, patterns, shapes, or position. A chart must remain readable in grayscale.

### Marks must meet contrast requirements

Data marks need 3:1 contrast against the background. Light fills meet this through their darker outline (see Fills and outlines). Text in charts follows standard text contrast (4.5:1).

### Provide a text alternative

Every chart needs a short summary of its key takeaway for screen readers. Charts with detailed data also need an accessible data table.

### Support keyboard access

Any information available on hover must also be available on keyboard focus.
